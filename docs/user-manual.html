Patch user manual

Patch brings your computer's audio into one place. Use it to choose where sound goes, balance applications and microphones, play music, mix DJ sets, record and broadcast.

Each part of Patch is called a Unit. Mixer, Router, Library and Recorder are Units. You can open one on its own or use a layout that opens several together. The audio service runs separately from those windows.

Start from the bar’s audio menu to open a layout. Press Ctrl+K in any Patch window to open the controller and choose Units, layouts or Setup.

Closing a window does not stop its audio. Decks, Player, recording and broadcasting have their own stop controls. At login, the audio service starts; Patch does not open windows automatically.

This first edition covers everyday use on Omarchy. Some Units depend on the modules you selected during setup. The detailed guides linked throughout cover advanced controls.

Patch’s main pink/red accent in Patch Dark is #F0243B (RGB 240, 36, 59).

1. Open Patch and choose a workspace

Open a layout from the menu bar

The bar's audio menu is the everyday starting point for Patch. A layout opens and arranges the Units you need for a particular job.

  1. Click the audio/volume icon in the Omarchy bar.
  2. Find the Patch section above the output devices.
  3. Choose a layout. Start with Simple for everyday audio: Mixer, Router and Player open together.
  4. Patch opens the layout on the next empty workspace.

Choosing a layout from this menu closes the existing Patch windows and opens the chosen arrangement. The audio keeps playing while you change layouts. You can switch from mixing to DJing without stopping the service.

The menu highlights the layout currently on screen. For that layout, use its actions to go to its workspace, Reset the window positions, Close its windows or save your arrangement. A dot indicates that the window arrangement has changed. Close all Patch windows closes Patch windows across workspaces; it does not stop playback, recording or broadcasting.

If the Patch section is missing, the bar integration may not be installed. See the bar's audio menu. You can still open Patch from the launcher as described below.

Use Ctrl+K to open the controller

With a Patch window focused, hold Ctrl and press K. This opens the Patch controller, your central place to:

  • Open an individual Unit, such as Mixer, Library or Recorder.
  • Choose a layout for the task you want to do.
  • Close Units or layouts.
  • Open Setup to choose modules and the default layout.
  • Check the audio engine's state.

For example, if you are DJing and want to check a recording, press Ctrl+K and choose Recorder. You do not need to replace the whole layout just to open another Unit. In the controller the arrow keys move and Enter opens; each Unit's letter, shown beside it, opens it directly (R for Recorder). Ctrl+Q closes all Patch windows; the music keeps playing. See Keyboard reference for every key.

Ctrl+K works inside Patch windows; it is not a desktop-wide shortcut. When no Patch window is open, use the bar's audio menu, or press Super+Space and choose Patch to open the controller. Layout launchers such as Patch Audio and Patch Auto Mix open their layouts directly.

Terminal equivalents:

omarchy-patch                      # open the controller

Press ? for the current window's help. Shortcuts depend on which window has focus; follow the controls and help shown there. Text fields keep their normal typing behaviour.

If a window says it is too small, choose Make room to float and enlarge it, or Keep small to accept its current size. Keeping it small can leave some controls out of view.

See Layouts for the full list and custom arrangements.

2. Get sound to your speakers or headphones

Start with a familiar audio source, such as a music file in Player or a song in your usual music application.

  1. Open the bar's audio menu.
  2. Choose the physical output you want: speakers, headphones or an audio interface.
  3. Check that the main volume is raised and unmuted.
  4. Start playback. Open Mixer and check for activity on the source's meter.

Patch follows the output selected in the bar. Choosing a physical device gives you the plain mixer. Choosing Patch exposes the full console and professional routing controls, retaining the current hardware output path.

Use Router to inspect connections. Routing means choosing a sound's source and destination; Mixer sets how much of that sound you hear. With Patch managing system audio, application sound continues through Patch even when a physical output is selected in the bar.

Main output and headphone cue

Main is the mix sent to your audience or speakers. Cue lets you check a track in headphones before bringing it into Main.

Independent Main and Cue need separate output ports. In Patch routing mode, assign Main and Headphone Cue to different ports or stereo pairs. They can use different pairs on the same audio interface. Cue needs a stereo pair, and its ports cannot overlap Main's.

Choosing a normal physical output in the bar disables the professional Cue routes. If you have only one stereo output, do not expect an independent headphone mix from it.

See Core routing for port assignments and capture routing.

3. Balance sound in the Mixer

A channel strip contains the controls for one sound source. Strips appear for actual application streams, assigned inputs and active Patch sources. A browser can supply several separate streams; Patch can control only the streams the browser makes available.

  1. Play the source you want to adjust and find its moving meter.
  2. Move that channel's fader to change its level in the mix.
  3. Use Mute to silence it. Check for active Solo controls if other sources unexpectedly disappear.
  4. Adjust Main for the overall listening level.

A meter shows signal level; a raised fader alone does not mean a source is playing. If levels clip or sound distorted, reduce the source or channel level. Making everything louder does not fix distortion already present in the source.

In the full console, Main, AUX and the effects returns sit at the right edge. An effects return carries processed sound back into the mix. Start with the source faders and Main before adjusting sends and returns.

Open EQ to change the tonal balance of an available channel. Select the channel you intend to change before adjusting it. Use bypass to compare the processed and unprocessed sound. Routing connections belong in Router.

Reading the meters

  • Peak shows short, high signal levels. Watch it for overloads.
  • RMS describes average signal level over a short period.
  • LUFS describes perceived loudness. Integrated LUFS covers the measured passage or take rather than just the latest instant.
  • dBFS measures digital level, with 0 dBFS at full scale.

Meter and Analyzer provide more detail than a strip's level meter. Use them when checking levels, stereo balance or frequency content.

4. Play a file or stream

Use Player for a file, a saved recording or an HTTP(S) audio stream. Use the DJ decks when you need cue points, beat matching or transitions between tracks.

  1. Open Player from the controller.
  2. Choose Open file, or enter a file path or stream URL.
  3. Use Play/Pause, seek and volume as needed.
  4. Enable repeat if you want a timed file to loop.

Player's shortcuts include Space for play/pause, O for the file chooser and L for repeat. Seeking requires a source with a known duration.

Stop rewinds a timed file; for a stream without a known duration, it closes the stream. Closing the Player window leaves playback running. Reopen Player to control it again.

See Recorder and Player.

5. Build your music library

The Library is your catalogue for DJing. Patch finds and analyses music in ~/Music. You can add other folders and removable drives as sources.

  1. Open Library.
  2. For a connected removable drive, use Add to library in Sources.
  3. Select a source to browse its collection, or browse all tracks.
  4. Let analysis finish before relying on automatic beat matching.

To add another folder from a terminal:

omarchy-patch library add "/path/to/music" "My music"
omarchy-patch library rescan
Track status What to do
Ready Ready for deck, sync and AI use; listen and check important tracks before a performance.
Check Read the reason and check the analysis, especially the beat grid. The file can still play.
Waiting Leave Patch time to analyse it.
Failed Read the error and check that the file is available and readable.

A beat grid marks where Patch thinks the beats and downbeats fall. Sync follows that grid. If the grid is wrong, two synced decks can still sound out of time. A confidence or readiness label does not replace listening.

Use Library search to narrow the list. In its keyboard controls, A/B load the selected track onto a deck (C/D onto decks C and D), Q queues it and Shift+C adds it to a collection. Confirm the destination deck before loading a track during a set.

Unplugged sources remain listed with their tracks offline. Reconnecting the drive makes those files available again. Removing a source from Library does not delete its music files. Patch does not write its analysis into those files.

See Library for file moves, duplicate copies and analysis storage.

6. Mix two tracks yourself

Open the Basic DJ layout. It puts Deck A, Crossfader, Deck B and Library together. Start at a comfortable listening level.

  1. Load a track onto Deck A and another onto Deck B.
  2. Set the crossfader towards A and start Deck A.
  3. If you have a separate headphone Cue output, cue Deck B in headphones.
  4. Find a suitable starting point on B. Check its tempo and beat grid.
  5. Use Sync if appropriate, then listen to the alignment.
  6. Start B at your chosen phrase and bring it into the mix with its level, EQ and crossfader controls.
  7. When B has taken over, pause A before loading its next track.

The crossfader balances the decks: left favours A, centre allows both and right favours B. Channel levels and EQ also affect what you hear.

Keylock keeps pitch steady while tempo changes. Cue returns to a saved starting point; hot cues recall other saved positions. Loop repeats a section. Check the window's help for the current controls before using them in a live set.

During a synced overlap, the incoming deck follows the outgoing deck. Once the handover finishes, the new lead deck does not need to remain synced to the paused deck. Its Sync button being off at that point is expected.

If Auto Mix is in Auto, moving a deck or mixing control takes over from it and returns it to Suggest. Check the mode before expecting automatic mixing to resume.

See DJ Booth for cueing, loops, EQ, effects and transport details.

7. Use the Auto Mix

Open the Auto Mix layout, or open the DJ workspace from the controller. Its main tabs are Play, Prepare, Recordings and Reports. In narrow windows, Prepare and Recordings may be labelled Sets and Takes.

The Auto Mix orders and crossfades tracks from your library, beat-matched and in key. It does not generate, compose or create music. It uses the library's analysis to choose the order and plan each transition. You choose how much control it has.

Mode What happens
Observe You operate the decks. Auto Mix listens and does not suggest or change playback.
Suggest Auto Mix ranks tracks. You choose, load and mix.
Prepare Auto Mix loads, cues and plans the next mix, then waits for your approval.
Auto Auto Mix chooses the order and runs the transitions. Some detail views call this ACT.

Start with Suggest to learn what it chooses and why. Expand Auto Mix detail to inspect candidates, reasons and the transition plan.

Prepare and start a set

  1. Open Prepare and name the set.
  2. Select its source collections. Selecting none makes the whole library available; selecting several combines them.
  3. Add an ordered track list if you want those tracks to play before automatic selections.
  4. Choose the duration, energy arc, tempo corridor and Auto Mix mode.
  5. Read the readiness checks, resolve missing files and Save.
  6. Choose Start set explicitly when you are ready to play.

Saving a draft does not start playback. Editing another draft while a set plays does not change the current queue.

Steer the set while it plays

  • Build / Hold / Release steer the energy direction. They do not directly set the tempo; an active set arc also guides the journey.
  • Change next rejects the current next choice and requests another.
  • Mix now / Skip requests a transition into the next track on a bar. It is not an immediate cut.
  • Queue arrows reorder pending tracks. Remove offers Undo.

Watch the actual loaded decks and next-track display. A Library row selection is a browsing choice, not proof of what Auto Mix will play next.

Pause, finish or take over

Action in the DJ workspace Result
Pause Pauses both decks and freezes the set arc, including an active transition.
Resume Continues the paused set.
End set Fades out and stops both decks.
Stop now Stops immediately.
Close the window Playback continues.

Touching deck or mixer controls takes over from Auto. Select Auto again when you want Auto Mix to resume. In Prepare, approve the prepared plan when you are ready for that mix.

Report a mix that went wrong

Select the transition and choose Report. Describe what you heard, such as poor timing, a clash or a level change, and add a note if useful. Reports retain information about that transition for review.

Replay and improvement evaluation belong in a review session with live playback paused or ended. A replay reconstructs the transition; a recording preserves what actually happened. Review and audition evaluated changes before activating them.

See DJ workspace and Auto Mix.

8. Record and listen back

Record a source with Recorder

  1. Open Recorder and select the source you want to capture.
  2. Choose a format. Use 24-bit PCM for a conventional high-quality WAV take; Float32 is also available.
  3. Start recording and check that its meter responds to the intended sound.
  4. Pause and resume if needed. Paused audio is omitted from the same take.
  5. Stop to save and finalise the recording.
  6. Open the saved take in Player and listen back.

Source and format stay fixed during a take. Closing Recorder does not stop capture. Check the take's clips, dropouts and any reported error after stopping. Do not assume a file is complete just because it appears in the folder.

Takes are saved in Patch Recordings under your Music directory, normally ~/Music/Patch Recordings. In a compact Recorder window, use Takes to find the saved files.

Choose the right source

Source Use it for
Program · Patch engine Patch's program mix before device/listening volume.
Patch bus Sound routed through the Patch bus.
What you hear · output The selected output device's monitor.
An application One available application's stream monitor.
Processed microphones · Patch Input The processed microphone capture mix.

These sources can contain different sound. Make a short test take and listen back before recording a full session.

Record a DJ set

Use Record program in the DJ workspace to capture the program mix with linked set and transition information. Set pause/resume coordinates that recording; ending the set finalises it. Find linked takes and export tracklists in Recordings. Pause live decks before playing back a take or rehearsal from that workspace.

See Recorder and Player and DJ workspace recordings.

9. Set up a microphone

Use headphones before enabling microphone monitoring through the main mix. Sending a microphone to nearby speakers can cause feedback.

  1. Open Mic and assign the physical microphone or interface input.
  2. New inputs start muted and unmonitored. Unmute the intended input when ready.
  3. Speak at your normal distance and watch the input meter.
  4. Start with a profile such as Natural, Podcast or Broadcast.
  5. Adjust software trim and processing while listening or checking a test take.
  6. In a calling or streaming application, choose Patch Input as its microphone.

To bring the far end of a call or an X Space into Patch, press + Call far end in Mic while it plays. The voice moves from its own strip into a Remote lane with the Remote voice profile. It stays out of Patch Input and every send (mix-minus), so the people in the call never hear themselves.

Use a browser voice as a mic

  1. Start playing the voice in Chromium.
  2. In Mic, click + Voice source. The Chromium strip in the Mixer shows which Voice lane now carries its sound.
  3. On that lane, turn the Aux1 send up to 0 dB.
  4. In the other browser, choose Patch AUX 1 as the microphone.

If Patch warns about a loop, use a different browser for the session that records AUX 1.

Speaker monitoring is a separate choice, Monitor in main mix. Leave it off unless you want to hear the microphone through Main. Turning down the speaker volume does not turn down Patch Input's capture mix.

The voice chain includes a high-pass filter, noise suppression, de-essing, gate, expander and compressor, followed by channel EQ. Start gently: too much gating or suppression can cut off quiet speech. Profiles are starting points and do not set an unidentified microphone's hardware gain.

Use Calibrate for a recorded comparison. Include room silence and normal speech, stop the take, then analyse it. Advice does not change the sound until you apply it. Save a named profile once the result suits your voice and room.

See Mic for profiles, calibration and processing controls.

10. Show visuals and broadcast

Visualiser

Open Visualiser from the controller, or use a layout that includes it. It follows the playing tracks, musical sections and DJ transitions.

Press F for fullscreen. L opens Layers for composable visual layers, including Decks & Signal and Performer. Use the window's help for scene, hold and mode controls. Visualiser generates its picture live; it is separate from recording or broadcasting audio.

See Visualiser and performance layers.

Broadcast

Open Broadcast to send an audio source to an Icecast server, an RTMP endpoint or a file.

  1. Select the source and destination type.
  2. Enter the destination details and credentials.
  3. Choose the encoding settings. RTMP uses AAC even if another codec is selected.
  4. Test with a file destination or your intended receiving service.
  5. Start and check for ON AIR, signal on the meter and successful reception.
  6. Stop explicitly when finished. Closing the window leaves it broadcasting.

RECONNECTING means Patch is trying to restore a previously working network stream. Audio during the outage is not sent. FAILED requires checking the reported error, destination and credentials.

Broadcast sends audio. To stream the Visualiser's picture as well, capture its window with a video application such as OBS. Keep stream keys private; the current encoder's destination credentials can be visible in its process arguments.

See Broadcast.

Network

Open Network (key Z) to send Patch's audio to other machines on your local network, or to play theirs through Patch.

  1. To send, pick the feed (Program, AUX 1 or AUX 2) and RTP or VBAN. Leave To empty to send to every machine on this network, or type one machine's address. VBAN always needs an address.
  2. Choose Send to everyone (or Send to …). The send shows SENDING once Patch's feed is linked to it.
  3. Streams other machines announce appear under Receive. Choose Listen: the stream plays in the Mixer like an app.
  4. Choose a higher Latency if a received stream crackles on Wi-Fi.

If a yellow box says the firewall drops network audio, choose Let it in from … and type your password in the terminal that opens. It opens Patch's ports to your own network only.

Sends and receives keep running when the window closes and start again with Patch's audio service. WAITING means Patch's engine is not running; the link continues when it is back. Stop a link with Stop, remove it with ✕.

See Network.

11. Save your workspace and settings

To change enabled modules or your default layout, open Ctrl+K → Setup.

To save your window arrangement, arrange the Units first, then run:

omarchy-patch layout save Night set

Reopen it with:

omarchy-patch layout night-set

Saving a built-in layout creates your own copy. Reset returns an arrangement to its saved positions. Closing a layout closes its windows and leaves audio running. A layout stores window placement; a saved DJ set stores your set's choices. They are separate.

Typical locations, with the default XDG directories:

Item Location
Recordings ~/Music/Patch Recordings
Music catalogue ~/.local/share/omarchy-patch/library.patchdj/
Analysis cache ~/.cache/omarchy-patch/analysis/
Configuration and custom layouts ~/.config/omarchy-patch/
Session state and transition reports ~/.local/state/omarchy-patch/

Keep your original music and recordings backed up. The analysis cache can be rebuilt; it is not a backup of those files.

12. Solve common problems

No sound

  1. Confirm that Player or a deck is actually playing.
  2. Look for signal on its Mixer channel.
  3. Check the channel's mute and fader, any active solos, and Main mute/volume.
  4. For decks, check channel levels and the crossfader position.
  5. Check the physical output selected in the bar and any professional port assignments in Router.
  6. If there is no live state, check the service from the controller or run omarchy-patch engine status.

A microphone works in Patch but not in a call

Choose Patch Input in the calling application's input settings. Check that the assigned microphone is unmuted and the capture mode is Inputs, rather than Main mix. Main listening volume and microphone capture volume are independent.

Two synced tracks sound out of time

Check the beat grids and downbeats, especially tracks marked Check. During an overlap, check Sync on the incoming deck. Listen for half- or double-tempo analysis. Sync aligns the analysed grids; it cannot establish that the detected grid matches the audible drums.

Auto Mix does not mix the next track

Check its mode first. Suggest waits for you; Prepare waits for approval. Manual deck or mixer movement may have taken over from Auto. Check for available, analysed tracks and read the displayed plan or readiness message. Enabling Auto-mix alone does not necessarily start a stopped deck; use Play or Start set.

Audio continues after closing Patch

Reopen the relevant Unit and stop its transport. For a DJ set, use Pause, End set or Stop now as appropriate. Stop Recorder and Broadcast separately when you finish those tasks.

Audio crackles or drops out

Check the device connection and lower unnecessary background load. Inspect Patch's engine status and health report:

omarchy-patch engine status
omarchy-patch health

The optional bar health dot is green when healthy, amber after a recent skip or a busy cycle, red after a very recent skip, and an empty ring when the engine is not running. Its tooltip identifies the condition. Enable it with omarchy-patch bar health on.

Keep the report and note what was playing, the output device and what happened just before the fault. See Performance for deeper checks.

A controller is detected but its controls do nothing

Open MIDI & HID, check whether a profile matched, and inspect its event monitor. Detection does not guarantee support for the device's protocol or mapping. The MIDI & HID Unit is different from the Patch controller opened by Ctrl+K.

Follow Controller setup for mapping and device checks.
